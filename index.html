01 — About

How I Think

I am drawn to questions that make people uncomfortable — about power, belief, consciousness, and human behaviour. Not to provoke, but because discomfort around a question is usually a signal that the question matters. That instinct shapes everything: how I read a research paper, how I listen to a conflict between two people, how I decide what to build.

When people disagree, I don't pick a side. I look for the missing piece of information — the moment where understanding broke down before anyone raised their voice. Find that moment, and the disagreement usually resolves itself. I've applied the same method to family conflicts, to geopolitical questions, and to datasets that contradict their own authors' conclusions. Same instinct. Different scale.

"I need to understand how the whole system works before I touch any single part of it."

I study computer science and psychology simultaneously because the most consequential AI understands humans — it doesn't just optimise for them. I have ADHD. For years I didn't have a name for what was happening — I just knew my brain wouldn't rest, wouldn't focus, wouldn't let me work the way I needed to. Then I found jazz. Then binaural beats. Then I ended up in a neuroscience lab watching EEG readings of brains responding to sound in real time — and I realised the technology that quietly reorganised my thinking doesn't reach most people. That is not an accident. That is a design failure. And design failures, unlike accidents, can be fixed.

Of 150 students who completed secondary school with me, I was the first to study in the United States. My oldest brother left school when our family needed him to — so that the rest of us could continue. Several of my closest friends from childhood never made it past middle school. One of them was a brilliant dancer — hired at thirteen to perform at weddings and celebrations, a talent that had nowhere to grow. That fact sits behind every decision I make about what to research, what to build, and who to build it for.

03 — Research

What I'm Building

Every project connects to the same root: making technology reach the people it hasn't reached yet — not as a statement, but as an engineering constraint.

Published · Peer-Reviewed IEEE Asia Pacific Conference on Wireless & Mobile (APWiMob) · Bali, 2025

Genomic Sequence Compression & Fragment Reconstruction Using Transformer Architectures

Clinical genomics has a silent data loss problem. Hospitals routinely lose genomic sequence information through storage corruption, transmission failures, and compression artifacts — and most existing systems can only do one thing: compress efficiently, or reconstruct accurately. Never both.

This paper presents GenomiCoder, a unified framework that solves both simultaneously. What made it novel: a k-mer-aware Transformer architecture with masked modelling and self-supervised pretraining — purpose-built for the biological structure of genomic sequences, not adapted from general-purpose NLP. The result outperformed every existing method across compression ratio, reconstruction accuracy, and robustness to corruption. Why it matters: in clinical genomics, a corrupted variant call isn't a data error — it's a missed diagnosis.

04 — Research Vision

Questions I Keep Returning To

Not research topics. The questions that won't leave me alone.

Can AI systems understand human emotional states without reducing them to data points?

The bridge between affective neuroscience and machine learning — where BCI meets clinical psychology. How you frame the measurement changes what can be measured.

How do we build healthcare AI that stays interpretable when a life depends on the explanation?

For clinical deployment, explainability isn't a feature — it is the precondition for trust. SHAP-based XAI as architecture, not an afterthought.

How do systems fail underserved populations — and how do we engineer that failure out?

Absent technology is not neutral. It is a choice embedded in every dataset, benchmark, and deployment decision. I understand what that choice costs.

What does computational biology lose when it optimises for storage efficiency over clinical fidelity?

Genomic compression that destroys variant information isn't compression — it is loss with institutional approval. The rate–distortion tradeoff has human consequences.

01

NeuroRoute — Hazard-Aware Emergency Routing

Standard routing treats all emergencies identically. NeuroRoute doesn't. A cardiac event and a paediatric emergency require different destinations — the system makes that distinction, applying weighted scoring per emergency type simultaneously across travel time, ER specialisation, hazard zones, and availability. 145 hospitals. 44 active hazard zones. Deployed live.

FlaskPythonLeaflet.jsOpenStreetMapJavaScriptPythonAnywhere
Child Emergency → Specialist Hospital Routing
Emergency → Nearest Police Station
Engineering Approach

Hazard-aware pathfinding evaluating each destination by travel time, distance, ER specialisation, hazard penalties, and emergency-type suitability — simultaneously. Graph-based weighted scoring, not simple proximity.

Stack & Deployment

Flask backend with JSON hospital and hazard datasets. Leaflet.js + OpenStreetMap for interactive map dashboard with live location, hazard visualisation, and route comparison. Deployed on PythonAnywhere.

CampusConnect — Desktop Social Network

Transfer students arrive on campus behind — no network, no shared context. CampusConnect was built to close that gap: authentication, real-time post feed, inbox messaging, and a relational SQLite backend. It didn't ship. It was the most instructive thing I built. Unfinished software has its own curriculum.

06 — Inner World

The Person Behind the Work

Research tells you what someone can do. This tells you how they see.

Songs that found me

Numb — Linkin Park   ·   ای ساربان — Ey Sareban (Persian)

"All I want to do / Is be more like me / And be less like you"

That line is not rebellion. It is the exhaustion of performing a version of yourself that others are comfortable with — day after day, without rest. The Persian song comes from somewhere older: a traveller asking the caravan to slow down because he is leaving behind everyone he has ever loved. Two songs from different centuries. One feeling. The cost of becoming yourself.

Artist

Michael Jackson

The biggest star in the world — and genuinely one of the loneliest people in it. What moved me was never the spectacle. It was the vulnerability underneath. He wrote about pain, belonging, and the need to be seen across every culture. Heal the World, Man in the Mirror, They Don't Care About Us. Empathy for the voiceless at a scale most people never attempt.

Idol

Bob Marley

He turned suffering into Redemption Song — not by erasing the pain, but by insisting that the people carrying it were worth singing about. One Love is not naive optimism. It is a radical refusal to accept that some people matter less. That refusal is something I recognise.

Book

The Brothers Karamazov — Dostoevsky

Three brothers who are each certain they're right about God, love, and morality — and all of them are, partially. Dostoevsky understood that the most honest questions have no clean resolution. Faith and doubt live in the same chest. I return to it when I need to remember that carrying a contradiction isn't confusion. Sometimes it's the most honest position available.

Film

Forrest Gump

Not the running. The whole arc. A man who never tried to be extraordinary just kept showing up — through grief, war, history, and loss — without pretending to understand the systems around him. He understood people instead. Quietly, that turned out to be enough. Presence over performance.

TV · Music · Sport

On sound and cognition

Why jazz became a research interest

I noticed early on that certain kinds of music — jazz, binaural frequencies — organised my thinking in ways that silence didn't. I became curious about why. That curiosity led me to neuroscience, to EEG readings in a lab, to watching brains respond to sound in real time. What I found there connected directly to the work I was already building toward.
